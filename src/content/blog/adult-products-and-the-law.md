This is not legal advice. It is a description of the limits on this website, and a reminder that the law at your delivery address is yours to know before you ask us to ship.

## Age

Every product here is for adults, 18 and older. The site asks you to confirm that before photographs are shown, and the order form asks again. We will not sell to anyone we believe is under 18. If you are unsure whether you are allowed to view this category where you are, stop and find out. Do not use the order form as the research.

## What we will not sell

We do not sell child-like figures, figures styled as children, or cartoon and anime lines. The collection is adult-realistic and starts at 157 cm. We do not add shorter bodies, and we do not keep a hidden category for them. Several places in the United States restrict child-like dolls specifically. A shop does not need to wait for one more statute to refuse that request. We refuse it. An inquiry that asks us to work around the rule will be declined, not negotiated.

The expression-head figure does not change the rule. Electronics do not make a child-styled product acceptable, and we do not offer one.

## Where the package goes

You are responsible for the law in the place a carton would be received. Some places restrict adult companion figures as such. We will not ship into a prohibition, and we will not misdeclare the contents to slip past one. That includes calling the figure a mannequin, a toy, or a dress form, and it includes grey routes and under-declared values. If the only way to deliver is a lie, there is no delivery.

Duties and taxes, where a lawful shipment owes them, are part of the destination’s rules. They are for the recipient unless we have agreed otherwise in writing. They are not a suggestion to hide the value.

## What a page on a shop can do

A product page can tell you the height, the material, the price, and that the expression head does not walk. It cannot tell you that your city, state, or country permits the import. It cannot tell you how a proposed bill you read about last year ended. If you need that answer, ask a lawyer where you live, not a checkout form. If we learn a destination is prohibited, we cancel the request rather than test the rule.

Privacy law is separate and written on the privacy page: what the browser stores, what you send if you email a request, and the rights people in places such as the EEA and the UK may have over information we actually hold. That page is about data. This one is about the product and the border. Read both if you are ordering across a border. Then send an order request only if both the product and the destination are ones we can handle in the open.

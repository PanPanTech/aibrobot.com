Choose a companion figure by the space it needs, the weight you can arrange to move, and the measurements of the exact body. Height and cup labels help describe its appearance, but the full table determines clothing, seating, support, and storage. Write down those requirements before narrowing the collection to a favorite face.

The current AIB Robot companion catalog spans 157 to 175 cm, with published body weights from 29 to 51 kg. The 164 cm expression-head figure has a separate listing whose weight still needs confirmation. Use the correct page for each candidate and ask for any missing measurement before payment.

## Read the numbers together

| Specification | What it helps you plan | What to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listed height | Overall scale, standing clearance, storage | How the height is measured for the offered configuration |
| Net body or complete-figure weight | Handling and support | Whether the stated weight includes the chosen head |
| Packaged weight and carton size | Delivery route, stairs, unpacking space | Current shipping dimensions and the available delivery service |
| Full bust, underbust, waist, hips | Clothing, seating, body proportions | Exact units and any measurements absent from the table |
| Shoulder, arm, leg, foot | Garment fit, shoes, support clearance | Measurements needed for the particular accessory |
| Cup label | The catalog's description of a body | Full bust and underbust where available |

## Height describes scale, not the whole shape

A 159 cm body can have a different torso, hip width, leg length, and weight from another body of the same height. The two 159 cm STPE listings in this collection illustrate the point. The C-cup and G-cup bodies share a height, but their published bust measurements and weights differ.

Height is still useful for planning. Measure the vertical clearance of the normal display position, including the support and footwear you intend to use. If the figure will usually sit, measure the chair, space for the legs, and depth needed in front of the seat. A seated arrangement changes the footprint significantly.

Check the route into that space. A doorway may be wide enough while the adjacent turn or staircase is restrictive. The shipping carton adds another set of dimensions. Request those dimensions when your home has a narrow entrance or limited unpacking room.

A product photograph can show a pose you like, but it does not show the complete room used to create it. Ask how that pose is supported and whether any visible prop belongs to the order. The [posing and storage guide](/blog/posing-stands-and-storage) covers support separately.

## Use cup labels with the full measurement table

A cup label is a model descriptor. It gives a quick idea of a body in the catalog, while full bust and underbust measurements provide more useful information for clothing. Do not transfer a familiar clothing size automatically to a figure with different proportions.

Some silicone companions in this collection are named by height and have a full measurement table without a cup label. That provides a workable basis for comparison. Copy their dimensions beside the STPE candidate using the same units and measurement names.

If you want a close-fitting top, request any missing underbust, shoulder, or arm measurement before purchasing it. If you want trousers, hips, waist, leg measurement, and the intended pose all matter. Begin with garments whose dimensions you can check and whose material is suitable for contact with the figure.

A fitted photograph may involve pins, stretch fabric, or an outfit chosen specifically for the shoot. It should not be read as a standard retail size recommendation. The [wig, makeup, and clothing guide](/blog/wigs-makeup-and-surface-aftercare) also explains dye transfer and accessories.

## Make handling arrangements before delivery

A companion figure is an awkward load with soft surfaces and joints. Holding a smaller dense object of the same weight does not necessarily reproduce the grip, balance, or reach needed to move the figure. Plan around the whole task, including the route and destination.

NIOSH's lifting guidance considers factors such as load weight, hand position, travel distance, frequency, and grip. It is occupational guidance for defined lifting tasks, which reinforces why one universal kilogram limit would be inappropriate here. [NIOSH lifting guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ergonomics/about/rnle.html)

Decide who will help, where the carton can rest, and how the figure reaches its normal supported position. If you need equipment or assistance, arrange it before the delivery. A temporary seat in the unpacking room can simplify inspection when a longer move needs a separate plan.

Use the handling instructions for the ordered figure. Hair, hands, wrists, and a neck connection should not become carrying handles. If the planned move feels uncertain, reconsider the body or the handling arrangement while you can still change the order.

## Distinguish net weight from shipping weight

A net weight may refer to a listed body, whereas a shipping weight includes packaging and may include accessories. Ask what each number covers. The difference matters when comparing a stand's suitability with a carrier's package description.

Likewise, a body measurement chart may have been recorded for a particular head or standard configuration. If the order changes that configuration, request confirmation of the relevant dimension. Keep the date of the chart in your notes, especially when different supplied documents give different values.

The 165 cm silicone listing, for example, uses a 32 kg figure from the 4 October 2025 sheet and notes that other records have given different weights. The useful next step is confirmation for the unit being supplied. Averaging those numbers would create a value with no corresponding product.

For the expression-head figure, the current site leaves the weight unlisted. Request it before deciding on support, handling, and delivery access. The ordinary 164 cm companion body's 33 kg value cannot answer that question.

## Check the skeleton and support options

The internal frame helps a figure hold a manually arranged pose. Its joint design and fitted options can differ between bodies or versions. Ask about the position you intend to use, especially if it involves long periods seated or supported standing.

Standing feet, a standing system, and a separate support stand are different configuration details that should be named individually. A photograph of a standing figure does not show the complete method holding it in place.

Record any essential support option in the inquiry. Ask which stand interface and handling method are approved for the exact body. Use the [skeleton and options guide](/blog/size-weight-and-internal-skeleton-options) to frame those questions.

Allow space for the stand base and any rear support. Furniture should be assessed against the total supported load and how the figure will contact it. The skin should not be trapped against a narrow edge or forced into an extreme pose simply to make the arrangement fit.

## Build a measurement worksheet for your room

Start with the intended location and write the available width, depth, and height. Add the chair or stand dimensions, access for cleaning, and the path needed to move around it. A closet that fits the figure tightly may still be inconvenient for care.

Next copy the candidate's height, weight, shoulders, hips, legs, and feet where relevant. Identify the numbers that are still unknown. Send that list with the product link instead of filling gaps from a nearby listing.

Then check the delivery route independently using the carton dimensions once supplied. Record door widths, staircase turns, and where the carton can be opened. Keep the packaging until inspection is complete and any transport concern is resolved.

Finally review your normal routine. If every clothing change requires moving the figure across a room, choose a more convenient location or a body whose handling requirements suit the help available. ## A practical choice between two heights

Imagine you are comparing the 159 cm C-cup figure with the 175 cm B-cup figure. The published weights are 36 kg and 38 kg respectively, so the taller option adds a relatively small difference in listed weight. Its height and body proportions still change the support and storage arrangement.

If your usual position is a supported seat beside a desk, compare chair depth, leg placement, and shoulder clearance. If storage is vertical, check overall clearance with the approved support. If delivery involves stairs, packaged dimensions and arranged assistance may become decisive.

Now compare the confirmed face, material, and complete price after those physical requirements are met. You can make a preference-based choice within a shortlist that already fits your home. That sequence reduces the chance of discovering a size problem after a sound delivery.

## Resolve photograph and chart disagreements

Some existing photographs include printed labels for a height, head, or skin tone. Where an overlay differs from the current page, send the discrepancy with your inquiry. Ask which body and head the quotation identifies and request current confirmation for the offered unit.

Keep the photograph, specification, and order confirmation connected in your records. If a measurement is essential to your furniture or handling arrangement, ask for it explicitly and state why it matters. The [listing checklist](/blog/what-a-listing-should-show) explains the information a product page and quotation should provide.

Once those details are resolved, save a copy of the final configuration and prepare the support before delivery. The useful size is the one that works in your space throughout ordinary ownership, including cleaning, dressing, storage, and any future move.

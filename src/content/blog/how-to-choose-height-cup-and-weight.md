A photograph of a figure in a doorway is not a floor plan of your doorway. Height, cup, and weight are the numbers that decide whether a companion figure is livable. This catalog starts at 157 cm and stops at 175 cm. Cups, where we list them, run from A to H. Weights run from 29 kg to 51 kg. Those ranges are wide enough to include a figure you cannot lift and a figure that will not pass a turn on your stairs.

## Height

157 to 159 cm is the shorter end of an adult-realistic collection. It is still a large object. 160 to 164 cm is a middle band that includes both a standard companion body and the separate expression-head figure, which is 164 cm and F-cup and does not walk. 165 to 169 cm starts to need real thought about ceilings, car seats, and closets. 170 to 175 cm is tall. The tallest companion in the list is 175 cm. At that height, even a modest cup is a lot of figure.

Do not shop “life size” as a feeling. Write down the vertical space you have when the figure is standing, and the horizontal space when it is seated. A seated pose shortens the height and lengthens the depth. A stand adds its own footprint.

## Cup and the rest of the table

Cup is a label on some bodies and absent on the silicone companions, which are described by height and a full measurement set instead. Where we print full bust, under bust, waist, hip, shoulder, arm, leg, or foot, those numbers come from the data file or the 2025 price list named on the page. Where a row was blank, we left it blank. A missing under bust is not zero. It is unknown until we confirm the unit.

Two bodies can share a height and differ in every other measurement. A 159 cm silicone companion and a 159 cm C-cup or G-cup STPE companion are not the same figure. Read the table before you treat the centimetres as the product.

## Weight

Weight is the number people skip and then regret on the stairs. Under 35 kg is still heavy for one person on a staircase. Around 38 to 40 kg is a two-person lift for many households. At 45 kg and 51 kg, which this collection includes, you need a plan before the carton is opened: who lifts, where it sits, and whether the stand is rated for that mass at that height. We print those warnings on the heavy product pages because a cheerful unboxing video will not carry the figure for you.

The expression-head figure does not have a published weight in the notes we are using. We confirm it before dispatch rather than copying the companion body’s weight onto a different product.

## Photographs

Use photographs for the face and the clothing, then return to the table. Some pack photographs include a printed overlay with a height, a head number, or a skin tone. The overlay can disagree with the listing. We do not edit those photographs to hide the overlay. If you see a number on the image that is not on the specification table, the table is what we are selling, and we will confirm the unit in writing.

Head pairing is an example unless we say otherwise. The head in a pack shot is a sample, not a promise that the warehouse unit wears that sculpt.

Choose the smallest figure that satisfies the look you want and that you can move without improvising. A taller figure is not a better figure. It is a heavier commitment.

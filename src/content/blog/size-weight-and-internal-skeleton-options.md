A companion figure's internal skeleton supports its shape and helps it hold a pose arranged by hand. To choose useful options, ask about the exact body's joint design, hands, foot configuration, approved support, and handling instructions. Read those answers alongside its dimensions and weight.

The current collection includes bodies from 29 to 51 kg, with different shapes and option records. The 164 cm expression-head figure is a separate configuration whose weight and body material need confirmation. A shared height cannot identify its internal construction or transfer another body's specifications.

## Match an option to a real task

| Your intended use | Detail to confirm | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mainly seated display | Approved sitting posture and support | Long-term pressure and joint position need planning |
| Supported standing | Foot configuration and compatible support | The body and external stand work together |
| Regular clothing changes | Joint handling and hand design | Fingers, wrists, and tight openings need careful handling |
| A particular hand pose | Fitted hand version and movement guidance | Small joints and soft fingers have their own limits |
| A different head | Connection and permitted head weight | Neck compatibility belongs to the complete pairing |
| Electronic expressions | Exact head and control system | Motorized head functions need separate specifications |

Use this table to describe your intended use in a [configuration inquiry](/contact). The more specific the pose or task, the easier it is to check whether the offered body is suitable.

## What the frame contributes

A soft body needs internal support to keep its overall form and hold manually adjusted positions. The frame, joints, surrounding materials, and external support all contribute to how it behaves.

A phrase such as articulated skeleton says little about the precise movement range at a knee, wrist, or neck. Ask for the approved handling guide for the exact version. If a demonstration is available, check that it shows the same body and fitted options.

Joint resistance can also vary with design. You should know how a joint is meant to be moved before applying force. An unexpected stop, looseness, or sound during setup deserves assessment while the original condition can still be documented.

Photographs show a finished pose. They rarely establish the forces used to arrange it or the supports outside the frame. The [posing and stand guide](/blog/posing-stands-and-storage) explains how to assess the complete arrangement.

## Keep weight attached to the exact body

The published examples in this catalog include a 157 cm B-cup figure at 29 kg, a 173 cm H-cup figure at 45 kg, and a 163 cm H-cup figure at 51 kg. Height alone would give a poor picture of their handling requirements.

A larger body volume, internal construction, and fitted components all contribute to the complete figure. Ask whether the quoted net weight includes the head and whether a requested change affects the number used for planning.

Keep packaged weight separate when discussing delivery. The carton, supports, and accessories affect the shipment, while a stand must be considered against the supported configuration. Request missing measurements before buying equipment.

Handling also depends on grip and reach. NIOSH's lifting material identifies factors beyond weight when assessing a task, including position, travel, and coupling. That context supports planning a complete move instead of using a single weight cutoff. [NIOSH manual overview](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/94-110/default.html)

## Hand options need a version and instructions

The supplied records include a ball-jointed hand description, and a silicone options sheet dated 4 October 2025 lists an updated hand option as free on that sheet. Current fitting and availability still need confirmation for the specific body.

Ask which hand version is fitted to a warehouse unit and which version can be supplied with an order. If the option matters to a planned pose, request a demonstration or close photographs of that version.

When dressing, support fingers and guide them through openings gently. Rings, bracelets, and tight cuffs can concentrate pressure on the surface. A realistic hand should be handled according to its construction, with attention to the small joints inside.

Record the hand option in the quotation along with any additional cost or included status. A historical sheet is useful background, while the written configuration identifies the hand you expect to receive.

## Feet and stands form a complete arrangement

The silicone options record lists normal and standing feet. Those labels describe a body option and should be confirmed separately from any external stand.

Ask how the fitted feet should be supported, which stand interface is suitable, and which positions are approved. A metal stand also needs a stable base, adequate height range, and contact points compatible with the figure.

A standing photograph may use a hidden support. Request the method used rather than reproducing the scene with whatever furniture is nearby. A narrow clamp can concentrate pressure even when the empty stand looks substantial.

Make room for the stand base and access for inspection. If the intended position puts the figure near a doorway or walking route, consider whether a supported seated arrangement would be easier to manage.

## Separate body articulation from head functions

The current expression-head figure combines a manually posed body with the limited head and neck functions described on its [product page](/ai-robots/wm-164-f-1068). Its electronic functions require the exact head version and supplied instructions.

If you are comparing it with a conventional companion body, ask separately about body support, head control, charging, and current network guidance. The ordinary 164 cm body's weight and measurement table belong to that listing.

A requested [voice box](/voice-box) also has its own function and compatibility. Include it as a named accessory in the quotation so the complete product is clear.

For a changed head pairing, use the [hybrid guide](/blog/hybrid-silicone-head-and-tpe-body) to check neck hardware, assembly, color, and component care together.

## Inspect function during the first setup

Place the figure in a stable supported position before exploring its movement. Keep the instructions nearby and begin with the movements they describe.

Move one joint at a time while supporting the adjacent body sections. Avoid pulling a limb from its far end or allowing the body's weight to load the neck during adjustment. Stop at unexpected resistance instead of trying to match a promotional pose immediately.

Photograph any concern before disassembly or attempted repair. A short description of the joint, direction of movement, and symptom helps support assess the problem. The [returns page](/returns) describes how to raise a defect or delivery issue.

Keep the original packing while the initial inspection is under way. The [first-week setup guide](/blog/first-week-unboxing-and-setup) provides the wider sequence for checking the figure and included components.

## Example of an option request

Suppose you want a figure seated most days, occasionally supported standing, and wearing long sleeves. Send the body link and explain those three requirements.

Ask which hand and foot versions are fitted, what support is approved for standing, and how the shoulders and elbows should be moved when dressing. Request the confirmed complete weight and the instructions that apply to that version.

Add the preferred head and hair type so the neck and handling advice covers the whole order. Keep the resulting answers with the [measurement worksheet](/blog/how-to-choose-height-cup-and-weight).

This gives each option a purpose. You can then compare an available warehouse unit with a proposed made-to-order configuration using the same requirements, supported by a clear written specification.

Care for TPE and STPE companion bodies with gentle material-approved cleaning, thorough drying, light handling and supported storage. Keep heat, unstable clothing dyes and concentrated pressure away from the surface. Choose powder or renewal oil only where the instructions for your exact unit recommend them. The care sheet supplied with that unit takes precedence over a routine borrowed from another figure.

TPE describes a family of thermoplastic elastomers. STPE is the material designation used for particular bodies in this catalog. A label alone cannot establish a universal cleaning recipe or an expected lifetime. A material compounder's technical comparison explains that chemical resistance varies among TPE formulations. That supports checking the actual compound's instructions before using a treatment. [Avient comparison of TPE and liquid silicone rubber](https://www.avient.com/sites/default/files/2023-06/AVIENT_TPEs%20and%20LSR%20eBook.pdf)

## Prepare a small, dedicated care kit

Keep clean soft cloths, a gentle approved cleaner, a drying cloth and any specified powder together. Label the cleaner and retain its instructions. Separate cloths used on the body from those used around paint, hair or accessories. A cloth that caught grit while cleaning a stand should be washed before touching a soft surface.

Prepare the figure's resting area before moving it. Use a clean padded support with enough room for the torso and limbs. Take off rings and check your fingernails for rough edges. Arrange help or suitable moving equipment if the weight or route is difficult. The [weight and skeleton guide](/blog/size-weight-and-internal-skeleton-options) explains why a poseable body still needs careful support.

## Clean in sections so the body remains supported

Wash your hands and inspect the surface before wetting it. Remove clothing carefully so that fasteners and jewellery cannot scrape the material. If the unit has electronics, follow its shutdown instructions and establish which areas must remain dry. Do not remove an electronic head unless the supplied procedure permits that operation.

Use lukewarm water and a mild unscented soap or the cleaner specified for the material. Apply it gently with a soft cloth, following the supplied dilution instructions. Work across a small section while the rest of the body stays supported. Strong scrubbing is a poor response to a stubborn mark because it can damage the finish before the mark changes.

Remove the cleaner thoroughly. Pay attention to folds and areas touching the support. Pat with a soft cloth and reposition carefully to inspect the underside. Avoid wringing, twisting or pulling the surface to reach a crease. Allow the figure to dry in a suitable room away from a heater and direct sunlight.

For any internal or removable component, use its specific care instructions. Ask for clarification if the instructions omit that part. This body-care routine does not establish that a cavity, insert, adhesive joint or electronic component tolerates the same cleaning method.

## Keep cleaning claims within the method used

Removing visible residue and claiming disinfection involve different methods. EPA explains that cleaning removes dirt and organic matter, while disinfectants have specific tested uses and label directions, including contact time. A bottle suitable for a household surface is not automatically appropriate for this figure. [EPA explanation of cleaning and disinfection](https://www.epa.gov/coronavirus-and-disinfectants/whats-difference-between-products-disinfect-sanitize-and-clean)

Avoid combining cleaners or adding a chemical to make an existing product stronger. CDC's general home-cleaning guidance advises following product directions, using a suitable method for the surface and keeping chemicals separate. Obtain both material compatibility guidance and the product's application instructions before using a disinfectant. [CDC guidance on safe cleaning](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-your-home.html)

## Use powder and oil deliberately

A dry elastomer surface can feel tacky. Where the unit's instructions recommend renewal powder, use the specified type and apply a light layer after drying. Brush away excess gently. Keep powder away from eyes, wig adhesive, seams leading to hardware and electronic openings. Applying it to damp material can leave patches that make later inspection harder.

Treat renewal oil as a material-specific maintenance product. Ask which formulation, amount and interval are approved before using it. Do not assume a product sold for silicone, skin care or another TPE figure is suitable. Excess oil can leave residue on clothing and supports, complicating the next cleaning cycle.

For example, if a body begins leaving more residue after a new treatment, record the product name, amount and timing. Stop adding further products and ask for guidance. Adding powder repeatedly may conceal the changing surface without explaining what caused it.

## Manage clothing, heat and contact pressure

Choose light, colour-fast clothing and inspect the inner seams, elastic and printed areas before dressing. Washing a garment can reduce loose dye, but it does not prove that prolonged contact is safe. Begin cautiously with any new fabric and inspect the surface when removing it. A dark garment that looks attractive in a short photograph may be a poor storage outfit.

Keep the figure away from direct sun, radiators, heating pads and hot vehicles. Let its storage room remain cool and dry. General heat-resistance figures for an industrial polymer cannot establish an acceptable treatment temperature for a finished body containing paint, adhesives and a skeleton.

Support broad areas rather than allowing a heel, elbow or folded joint to carry the load for weeks. Check the stand's documented capacity, fitting method and compatibility with the body. A seated position also needs support that avoids hard edges pressing into the surface. Review the [posing and storage guide](/blog/posing-stands-and-storage) before adopting a long-term arrangement.

## Inspect changes before attempting a repair

Routine inspection works best when it is consistent. Look at the neck, shoulders, finger bases, joints, seams and areas resting on furniture. Compare a suspected change with a clear earlier photograph under similar light. Ordinary surface residue, a transferred dye mark and a structural opening need different responses.

If a tear or seam opens, reduce movement at that area and photograph it with a scale nearby. Record when you first noticed it, any recent handling and the products used. Send those details through the agreed contact process before using glue, heat or a repair kit. Keep tools and improvised solvents away from the affected area while it is assessed.

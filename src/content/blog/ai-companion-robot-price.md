The [AIB Robot expression-head companion](/ai-robots/wm-164-f-1068) is listed at **$3,099** as of 15 November 2026. The optional [voice box](/voice-box) is **$100**, pending confirmation, and serves a different purpose. To calculate what you would pay, start with the confirmed configuration, add separate accessories and freight, and identify who pays any taxes or clearance charges. Check future service and maintenance costs separately.

For a posed companion, part of the comparison is physical. Weight affects handling, skin material affects care, and a specific head or finish may affect which warehouse units fit your needs. The [material comparison](/blog/tpe-vs-stpe-vs-silicone) explains what to check. For the expression-head figure, confirm which functions the head adds and how they work at your destination. The body is posed by hand. Its range of movement is described in the [expression robot guide](/blog/ai-bionic-and-expression-robots).

Consider a specific quote calculation. A $2,199 STPE figure plus a confirmed $100 voice box would have a $2,299 item subtotal if the box fits, both prices still apply and the quote lists no other item charge. Freight and destination charges still need their own entries. An audio-equipped figure and an expression-head configuration have different feature sets, so compare the behavior you would actually get at each item subtotal.

## Specify the item before comparing quotes

Start with the catalog name and model identifier. Record the head, body material, height, finish and any selected options. Then record what you want the electronics to do. “Play included audio using an on-device control” and “accept a spoken question and answer in English over my home network” are distinct requirements. Ask which component supplies each function and whether the seller can demonstrate that behavior on the version being quoted.

For any audio accessory, ask whether it will arrive installed, which parts are included and how you would reach the controls or clean around the module. Request the item subtotal with and without the accessory. If one function determines whether you would buy the figure, ask how the seller handles a configuration that fails to perform that function in the agreed setup. Save the reply with the exact model and version.

The product page for #1068 gives 164 cm and F-cup, with material and further measurements subject to confirmation. Ask for the actual net and packed weight if you need to plan the lift, arrange a delivery appointment or choose a storage stand. Figures of the same height can have different builds, so use the written size and weight for the configured unit. The [size and skeleton guide](/blog/size-weight-and-internal-skeleton-options) explains what to request.

## Calculate your delivered total

Record the amount due to the seller and any amounts you may pay separately. Useful line items include the figure, accessory and fitting, freight, destination tax or duty and any separate carrier or brokerage fee. For an international payment, ask which currency appears on the invoice and check the exchange rate and fees with your payment provider. Keep unknown charges open on your worksheet until you find out who can confirm them.

| Quote line | What to record | Where to confirm it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Figure and included parts | Model, head, material, finish, controls, cables and other included items | Itemized quote |
| Options | Module price, installation and requested changes | Configuration section of the quote |
| Shipping | Actual origin, destination postal code, service and price | Seller or named carrier |
| Taxes and import charges | Included amounts, responsibility and separate clearance fees | Seller's terms, carrier and destination authority |
| Currency and transfers | Invoice currency, conversion and payment-provider charges | Invoice and payment provider |

Confirm shipping origin for that individual order. The November 2026 warehouse lists used by AIB Robot do not show the #1068 configuration in the US or the EU. Ask for the available-to-order lead time, where the confirmed unit would ship from and what the quote covers. The [shipping page](/shipping) explains how AIB Robot handles freight and clearance discussions. Readers buying in Europe can refer to the [EU buying guide](/blog/buying-a-companion-figure-in-the-eu) for destination and tax questions.

Check where the carrier service ends. Building access, stairs, a door threshold and the ability to receive the carton can affect the handling you arrange yourself. Request carton dimensions and packed weight. If you need an appointment or help beyond the carrier's delivery point, get a separate quote for that service and put it in your budget. Keep the delivery record and carton details so you can identify the shipment if something arrives damaged.

## Budget for the first year of ownership

Separate basic care and support from optional spending. You may already have a soft, suitable cleaning cloth and a protective surface. Other supplies depend on the model, finish and care instructions. Measure the storage space and verify supported contact points before buying a stand. If you will store and display the figure in different places, consider how you can move it between those locations safely. The [care guide](/care) covers the materials in the catalog.

Electronic costs depend on the supplied component. Ask about the charger, connector and availability of replacements. If speech or expression relies on a remote service, request its account terms, including any trial, price and billing frequency. If you depend on the feature, ask what happens if a service is interrupted. Keep possible repair expenses separate from the quoted upfront cost. Confirm whether the head or module can be serviced independently and who pays shipping for an agreed repair.

Separate options you can buy later from parts that require factory fitting. A compatible spare charger and a head-specific installation service have different timing. If you want both, ask when they can be supplied and who has to fit them. A written answer gives you a basis for deciding which expenses belong in the order and which you can defer.

## Compare a warehouse offer with a made-to-order quote

Treat the November warehouse lists as a starting point. For an item shown as stocked, ask which unit the seller can allocate today. Match the head code, finish, carton weight and any photos to that specific unit. A dated list tells you where to start, but a recent confirmation tells you what can actually ship. Ask whether an accessory can be fitted before it leaves the warehouse or whether adding it changes the source or lead time. The [stock and made-to-order guide](/blog/in-stock-vs-made-to-order) gives a checklist for that decision.

A made-to-order quote needs a different delivery record. Ask when assembly can start, when a configuration review can happen and when the seller can give a freight quote based on the completed package. Write down which figures are fixed at payment and which details may be confirmed closer to dispatch. If an electrical or regional requirement is unresolved, agree when you will get an answer and what happens if the seller cannot supply the needed configuration.

Compare the cost on the same basis. For example, if one quote ends at door delivery with destination charges collected separately and the other lists prepaid destination charges, collect the separate amounts before comparing the totals. Use the same approach when comparing an installed accessory to one arriving loose. Include the installation you would need in the loose module's ownership costs.

## Ask for a quote you can compare

Send the destination, model, required functions and options in one request. Ask how long the quote remains valid and when any open specification will be confirmed.

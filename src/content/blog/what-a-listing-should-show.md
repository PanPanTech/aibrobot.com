A clear listing is boring in a useful way. It names a height, a material, a price with a date, and what is not included. A vague listing leans on a category word — robot, realistic, in stock, AI — and asks you to pay before those words are tied to a unit. This article is about reading the difference. It is not a directory of who is allowed to sell, and it does not rank manufacturers.

## Price with a date

On this site, STPE companions are shown at $2,199 beside a previous list price of $2,599, as of 15 November 2026. Silicone companions use suggested retail from a 4 October 2025 list, $1,900 or $1,950. The expression-head figure is listed at $3,099 as of the same November date. The voice box is $100, pending confirmation. Those are public figures. They are reconfirmed before you are asked to pay. A crossed-out number is a previous list price, not a coupon we invented for the week.

If a page shows a dramatic discount with no date and no statement of what the number used to be, ask what the number is. If the answer is a mood, do not pay. We do not publish trade prices, cost prices, or a count of units in a warehouse. A retail page that offers those as a lure is offering something this site will not match, because we do not have them to publish.

## Stock that is a list, not a promise

“US warehouse” on this site means the body appeared on a list we received in November 2026. It does not mean we have counted the shelf this morning. Before payment we ask whether a unit is still there and which head, skin, and material are actually on it. “Available to order” means the figure is not on those lists. Lead time is written for your configuration. We do not publish a delivery-day range copied from someone else’s FAQ.

The expression head is available to order even if a similar companion body is on a warehouse list. Those are different products. Marking the robot in stock because the body exists would be a false listing. We will not do it.

## Photographs

A photograph should be labelled as a pack shot, a warehouse shot, or collection photography that is not a specific figure. The head in a pack shot is an example pairing unless the page says the head is fixed. Some images carry a printed overlay. We leave the overlay in place. If it names a different height than the specification table, the table wins, and you should notice the disagreement before you order rather than after.

Collection photographs of clothing, used on the home page and the journal, are not a figure you can add to the inquiry bag. If a shop’s only images are lifestyle crops with no measurements, you do not yet have a listing.

## The order itself

Checkout here is an order request. Nothing is charged on the page. A contract starts when we accept a confirmed order in writing, after inventory and the final price are agreed. You should see your name, the destination, the figure, the material, any voice box, and the indicated total, and you should be told that the total can change if the unit or the freight changes. You should not be asked for a card number in email.

You should also be told what we will not do. We will not misdeclare a carton. We will not ship where the product is prohibited. We will not sell a child-like or anime figure. A seller who treats those limits as negotiable is telling you how the rest of the order will be handled.

## A five-line test

Before you pay anyone, including us, you should be able to write:

1. The height, material, and price, with the date of the price.
2. Whether the unit is on a named warehouse list or still has to be made.
3. What the photograph is, and what it is not.
4. What the figure does not do, if it is being sold as a robot.
5. How you get your money’s remedy if it arrives damaged.

If line 4 is “it walks” for the expression head we sell, the listing is wrong. That figure does not walk, and its home-network behaviour is confirmed before dispatch rather than promised in a headline.

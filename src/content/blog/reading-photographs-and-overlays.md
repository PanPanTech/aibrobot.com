Photographs sell this category, and photographs are also where listings go wrong. A figure is posed, dressed, lit, and sometimes labelled in the corner of the frame. The label can name a height, a head, or a skin tone. The face in the frame may be a sample. None of that is a reason to throw the photograph away. It is a reason to read it as a photograph.

## What we keep, and what we do not retouch

Product images on this site stay with the figure they were supplied for. Where a pack photograph has a printed overlay, we have left it there. We do not paint over labels, and we do not claim a clean studio original we do not have. If you can see type in the corner, you are seeing the file we received.

That honesty creates a second job for the product page. The specification table is the listing. The caption says when the head is an example pairing. On the 164 cm F-cup companion and on the expression-head figure, some photographs carry an overlay that does not match the height in the table. We say so on the page. Use the table. If you want the overlay’s number instead, you are looking at a different product than the one we are offering, and you should tell us before you order rather than assume we will substitute it.

## Sample heads

A body is often photographed with a head that happens to be on it that day. Hair, eye colour, and makeup travel with that sample. Your order may confirm a different head. Implanted hair, a wig, and eye colour are options on the silicone companions, with prices on the 2025 sheet, and they are requests until we confirm them. Do not fall in love with a hairline and then discover it was a wig in the sample.

Collection photographs elsewhere on the site — a jacket, a magazine, a denim outfit — are not assigned to a figure. They show clothing and finish. They are labelled that way. If you arrived from one of those images, go to the shop and read a table before you add anything to the inquiry bag.

## Lighting and clothing

Soft light hides seams. Dark clothing hides the surface you will actually clean. A figure photographed outdoors in jeans is the same material when the jeans come off. TPE and STPE can look smoother in a photograph than they feel after a week without powder. Silicone can look more “plastic” or less, depending on the highlight. The photograph is not a material test.

Clothing in a sample may also be dyed in a way you should not copy. Unstable dyes stain elastomer. The outfit in the photo is not a care recommendation.

## A small habit

Look at the photograph once for the face, then look at the table, then look at the photograph again and ask what it cannot tell you. It cannot tell you the weight if the table has a weight and the photo does not. It cannot tell you the head is fixed if the caption says it is an example. It cannot tell you the figure walks. It cannot tell you a network will work in your house. Those sentences live in the text, and the text is allowed to be less flattering than the picture. That is the point of having both.

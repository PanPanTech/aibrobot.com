Read a companion-figure photograph as evidence of the photographed appearance. Use the specification table and written confirmation to identify the item offered. Printed overlays, sample heads, clothing and lighting can all create details that need a separate answer before you order.

AIB Robot keeps supplied product photographs with the figures they were provided for and retains visible printed overlays. Some overlays disagree with the current listing. Gallery captions explain example head pairings, and collection images illustrate appearance without identifying a particular order. The following method helps you use those images carefully without losing the details that first interested you.

## Identify what kind of image you are viewing

Begin with the image's role. A full product photograph may show a posed body with a sample head. A warehouse image may document a particular configuration at the time it was taken. A close portrait may show only a face and hair. A collection scene may illustrate styling.

Those roles establish different kinds of evidence. A gallery can help you ask about proportions, joints or makeup, while a close portrait leaves the full body outside the frame. A warehouse photograph still needs to be connected to the available unit and the current confirmation.

| What you see | What it can help you examine | What still needs a written answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full product photograph | Visible proportions, pose and photographed head | Actual material, measurements and head supplied |
| Close face portrait | Hairline, makeup and visible sculpt details | Exact head, wig or implanted-hair option |
| Printed height or head overlay | Information embedded in the supplied file | Whether it matches the current listed configuration |
| Dressed collection scene | Styling, clothing arrangement and overall appearance | Product identity and included clothing or props |
| Electronic-head diagram | Locations or parts labeled in that diagram | Actual hardware, operating instructions and supported functions |
| Warehouse photograph | Appearance of a unit at the time photographed | Current availability and connection to the accepted order |

## Compare the overlay with the specification table

A printed overlay is part of the image file. It can contain a body height, a head identifier or a material description. Retaining it lets you see what the original photograph said, but the overlay may belong to a different pairing or catalog description.

On some 164 cm F-cup companion and expression-head photographs, the printed overlay differs from the height in the product table. The table identifies the listing offered by AIB Robot. If you intended to buy the size printed in the photograph, raise that point before proceeding.

Keep the discrepancy precise. Save the image and write down the conflicting detail, such as the printed height and the listed height. Ask which body is being offered and request confirmation of the dimension that affects your decision. Avoid resolving a conflict by choosing whichever number you prefer.

If the written answer changes the product configuration, check the associated price, material and availability again. A different body can affect more than the label. Our [listing guide](/blog/what-a-listing-should-show) explains how to follow those changes into a complete offer.

## Treat a sample head as a starting point

A photographed body may be paired with a head selected for that shoot. The head, makeup, wig and eyes can strongly influence how the figure appears, even when the underlying body remains the same.

Check the caption for example-pairing language. If a particular face matters, describe it using the image and any visible head identifier. Ask whether that exact pairing can be supplied, whether it is already on a warehouse unit, and whether the finish matches the photograph closely enough for your expectations.

Hair deserves its own question. A wig and implanted hair can produce different hairlines and maintenance needs. The silicone options sheet includes separate hair choices, but availability and pricing need current confirmation. A close photograph cannot by itself tell you which option is included in the public figure price.

Eye color and makeup should also appear in the final configuration if they determine your choice. A general request for the same look may leave too much open to interpretation. Refer to the actual detail you want and keep the response with the image.

## Allow for lighting and image processing

Light changes the way a surface appears. A strong highlight can make skin look glossier, while a broad soft light can reduce the visibility of contours. Dark clothing or deep shadows can conceal areas that you would want to inspect before buying.

Color can also shift through white balance and image adjustments. Adobe explains how lighting and white-balance settings affect the color recorded in a photograph. This is a reason to confirm a named skin-tone option and the actual configuration before treating an image's exact hue as a specification. [Adobe white-balance guide](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/discover/white-balance.html)

A different-looking color across two images does not establish that one is deceptive or that the material changed. Ask whether they show the same body, head and finish and whether the lighting differs. Where a surface detail matters, request a suitable view if available.

A photograph also cannot establish softness, weight or the care routine. Those require the material description, measurements and supplied instructions. Use the [materials page](/materials) to understand the available material labels, then confirm the one chosen for your order.

## Check clothing, props and supports separately

An outfit can change the apparent body proportions and hide a joint or seam. Look at the available gallery as a whole before deciding whether it shows the areas you care about. If those areas remain covered, ask a specific question about their construction or appearance.

The pictured clothing, wig, jewelry, magazine or support may have been used for styling. Ask whether any such item is included rather than relying on its presence in the frame. Record an agreed inclusion in the same order confirmation as the body and head.

Do not use a photographed outfit as a care instruction. Unstable dyes can stain elastomer surfaces, and the suitability of clothing needs its own assessment. Check the [care guidance](/care) before leaving clothing in contact with the figure for extended periods.

A pose likewise needs the right support and joint handling. A standing or seated photograph establishes the position used for that image. It provides no instruction to balance the figure unattended or force a joint to recreate the pose.

## Keep photographs separate from function evidence

A still image cannot demonstrate conversation quality, facial movement or battery runtime. Decorative glasses, futuristic clothing or a technical-looking background add styling without establishing electronics inside the photographed figure.

The expression-head listing describes limited head and neck motion on a body that is posed by hand. It cannot walk. Its body photographs use example pairings, while diagrams supplied with the head have a different explanatory role. Read the current product description for capability limits.

If a demonstration prompted your inquiry, identify the model and the function shown. Ask whether that function belongs to the configuration being offered. A product photograph and a video of a different electronic system should not become a combined feature list in your notes. The [expression-head guide](/blog/ai-bionic-and-expression-robots) explains the documented scope.

## Use a repeatable three-pass review

On the first pass, note what you like about the appearance. Be concrete about the face, hair, proportions or clothing. That gives your inquiry a useful starting point.

On the second pass, read the specification table and every relevant caption. Record the height, material, head status, measurement source and any overlay disagreement. Mark details that the image cannot answer, including weight or inclusion of an accessory.

On the third pass, return to the photographs with those questions in mind. Choose the exact image that illustrates each question. One clearly referenced image is easier to answer than an instruction to match the entire gallery.

For example, you might like the hairstyle in a companion portrait but be selecting an available warehouse body. Ask whether that head and hairstyle are on the current unit or require a separately confirmed change. Keep the current stock answer and the appearance answer together before accepting a quotation.

The result is a visual reference connected to a product record. Save that record with the final configuration so you can check the delivered figure against the actual agreement and recognize which details were illustrative from the start.

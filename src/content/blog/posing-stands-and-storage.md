Plan a companion figure's pose around its approved movement range, confirmed weight, and a suitable support. For storage, leave the surface clean and fully dry in a cool, shaded place, with the body supported and pressure points easy to inspect. Prepare that arrangement before unboxing a full-size figure.

The current AIB Robot collection includes bodies at 45 and 51 kg. Even a lighter figure can be awkward to move because of its length, soft surface, and joints. A stable everyday location reduces unnecessary handling and makes cleaning, dressing, and inspection easier.

## Choose an arrangement you can maintain

| Arrangement | Check first | During use or storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supported seated display | Chair strength, seat depth, contact surfaces | Look for concentrated pressure and unsuitable folds |
| Supported standing | Approved foot option and compatible stand | Keep the base stable and inspect contact points |
| Reclined storage | Body and head support appropriate to the design | Keep limbs and neck from carrying unintended loads |
| Covered display | Clean, dry cover and room ventilation | Check the surface before covering and after reopening |
| Long pause in use | Material instructions and electronics storage guidance | Review condition regularly and follow battery instructions |
| Moving between rooms | Clear route, assistance, and handling method | Prepare the destination before beginning the move |

Use the [height and weight guide](/blog/how-to-choose-height-cup-and-weight) to identify the actual dimensions of your figure. The support method should suit the complete configuration, including any electronic head.

## Prepare the position before moving the figure

Choose a level place where the stand or furniture can remain stable. Check the space around doors, heaters, direct sunlight, and normal walking routes. Allow room for you to work around the figure without reaching over an unstable support.

Set out clean cloths and the approved care products before the move. Remove sharp jewelry or accessories that could catch the skin while you handle the body. Keep the destination free of loose objects that could become pressure points.

Arrange help or suitable equipment if the route or load is difficult. Consider the full move, including turns, stairs, reach, and where you will put the figure down if you need to pause. Occupational lifting guidance from NIOSH considers several task variables alongside weight, which is a useful reason to plan the route carefully. [NIOSH lifting guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ergonomics/about/rnle.html)

Follow the supplied handling instructions for the figure. Support its major body sections and protect the head connection. Hair, wrists, hands, and ankles should not become carrying handles.

## Adjust joints with the adjacent sections supported

Begin from a stable supported position. Move one joint at a time and watch the surrounding skin as the angle changes. A pose can look comfortable in a photograph while requiring a different support arrangement on your figure.

Stop at unexpected resistance, looseness, or an unusual sound. Record the joint and symptom before trying again. If a movement is unclear, ask for the instructions or a demonstration for that exact body.

Take particular care with fingers during dressing, and with the neck while adjusting the head. Avoid pulling from the end of a limb when the movement is intended at another joint. The [skeleton and options article](/blog/size-weight-and-internal-skeleton-options) explains what to confirm about hands, feet, and frame versions.

Once the pose is set, inspect contact points and any tightly folded areas. A supported position should allow you to check the surface without repeatedly lifting the whole figure.

## Assess the stand as equipment

A stand needs a suitable load rating, height range, stable base, and compatible contact system. Obtain those details from its maker and ask whether the design is approved for your specific figure.

Weight is one part of the assessment. The height of the supported load, base footprint, and position of the support also affect stability. Furniture or a lightweight photographic stand should be assessed for the actual task rather than selected because it resembles a prop in a picture.

Check how the stand contacts the figure. A narrow edge or excessive clamp pressure can concentrate load on soft material. Follow the approved fitting method and inspect the area after setup.

The current silicone options record includes normal and standing feet. Confirm the fitted option separately from the stand itself. A standing-foot option should be discussed as part of the complete supported arrangement.

## Select furniture and clothing for contact

A chair or bench should support the total load and the intended pose. Check edges, fasteners, rough upholstery, and anything sharp beneath a cushion. A surface that feels smooth under clothing can still concentrate pressure over time.

Light, color-fast fabric is a sensible starting point for contact with the figure. Dark clothing, printed textiles, and accessories may transfer pigment. Follow the material care instructions and check the surface regularly when introducing an outfit.

Tight straps, jewelry, shoes, and waistbands can mark a soft surface when left in place. Loosen or remove items for longer storage where the guidance recommends it. Keep damp garments away from the figure and dry removable clothing fully before refitting.

The [hair, makeup, and clothing guide](/blog/wigs-makeup-and-surface-aftercare) gives a more detailed method for introducing new styling materials.

## Put the figure away clean and dry

Use the routine approved for the exact material. Complete drying matters before a cover, garment, or storage enclosure limits air movement around the surface.

For an elastomer figure, apply a recommended renewal product only as directed. Avoid adding oil simply to prepare for storage. Keep powder and liquid away from eyes, hair attachments, connectors, speakers, and ports.

Choose a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and heaters. A clean breathable cover can protect from dust while allowing inspection. Check the inside of the cover for unstable dyes and rough seams.

Follow the supplied guidance on a supported resting position. Keep the head and limbs from hanging in ways that load joints unnecessarily. Review the surface periodically rather than assuming a figure can remain in any position indefinitely.

## Store electronic components using their instructions

For the [expression-head figure](/ai-robots/wm-164-f-1068), use the supplied power-down, charging, and storage directions for the exact version. Ask about the recommended state of charge and inspection routine before a long period out of use.

Keep components and connectors dry. Route cables so they cannot be pulled when the figure or support moves. Use the charger supplied or approved for the unit.

The optional [voice box](/voice-box) has its own access and care requirements. Record those with the body instructions so cleaning and storage remain compatible with the fitted module.

If liquid reaches an electronic area or a connector appears damaged, stop using it and request guidance. A surface cleaning mistake should be assessed before charging or testing the device.

## A practical room setup

Suppose your figure will usually sit beside a bookcase and occasionally be dressed for photographs. Choose the supported seat first, check its dimensions and strength, and allow room around it for changing clothes.

Keep a clean nearby surface available for the approved care routine and arrange how the figure will reach it. Store care products, spare wigs, and accessories separately so they cannot leak or press against the body.

For a longer pause, remove any damp or tight clothing, complete the material-specific care routine, check the joints and contact areas, and fit a suitable clean cover. Keep electronic instructions accessible with the charger.

When you next uncover the figure, inspect the skin, support, hair, and connectors before moving it. Use the [first-week inspection sequence](/blog/first-week-unboxing-and-setup) as a reference. If you find damage or an unexpected change, photograph it and contact support through the [returns process](/returns) before attempting a repair.

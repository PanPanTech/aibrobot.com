Clean a silicone companion figure using the cleaner and method approved for its finished surface, support its weight during handling, and keep prolonged pressure, unstable dyes and strong heat away from it. Inspect joints and contact areas regularly. These habits make condition easier to manage, while a fixed lifespan in years would require evidence this collection does not have.

The body material, surface paint, hair attachment, eyes and internal skeleton need to be considered together. An industrial silicone's chemical resistance cannot establish compatibility for every layer of a finished figure. Avient's material comparison describes different performance characteristics for TPE and liquid silicone rubber, including chemical exposure. Use that distinction to ask specific questions about your unit. [Avient TPE and silicone comparison](https://www.avient.com/sites/default/files/2023-06/AVIENT_TPEs%20and%20LSR%20eBook.pdf)

## Identify what you are caring for

Save the material confirmation, fitted head details and supplied care instructions with the order. A silicone head can be paired with a different body material. That combination needs separate instructions for each surface. Read the [hybrid-head guide](/blog/hybrid-silicone-head-and-tpe-body) if your chosen configuration has mixed materials.

For the current expression-head figure, the notes differ about the body material. Obtain a unit-specific confirmation before adopting a silicone routine. The head contains electronics and moving mechanisms that need their own instructions even if some of its external skin is silicone. The [AI product page](/ai-robots/wm-164-f-1068) records the current configuration limits.

## Plan a cleaning session around the weight

Place clean cloths, the approved cleaner and a drying cloth within reach. Prepare a padded resting area wide enough to support the torso and limbs. Remove rings and accessories that could catch the surface. Plan how to turn the body with suitable assistance if you cannot keep its weight supported comfortably.

Check the neck, shoulders, fingers, knees and resting surfaces before cleaning. Photograph a suspected opening or unusual mark while it is dry and clearly visible. If a structural area looks damaged, reduce movement there and request guidance before continuing. Cleaning should not require forcing a stiff joint or pulling on a limb.

| Part or area | Practical care approach | Stop and ask when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad body surface | Approved cleaner, gentle wiping and controlled rinsing | Paint transfers or the surface changes unexpectedly |
| Folds and joint areas | Support the joint and remove residue carefully | A seam opens or material catches during movement |
| Painted face | Use the specified method for that finish | A colour change persists after gentle cleaning |
| Hair and wig attachment | Follow the supplied brushing and attachment notes | The base or implanted area becomes loose |
| Electronic openings | Keep dry and use the component instructions | Liquid enters, a port is damaged or operation changes |
| Support contact areas | Inspect for concentrated pressure and hard edges | An indentation or cut develops |

## Clean gently and remove residue

Use the product's approved silicone cleaner, or the gentle method specified in its care sheet. Lukewarm water and a soft cloth are part of the routine described on this site's [care page](/care). Follow the cleaner's directions for dilution and removal. Work in sections, keeping the figure stable and protecting openings from runoff.

Rinse or remove the cleaner as directed, then pat dry. Check beneath the torso and around folds before dressing. Avoid twisting the surface or holding an unsupported limb away from the body to reach underneath. Reposition the whole figure with appropriate support instead.

This routine does not authorise strong solvents, abrasive pads, alcohol or acetone. Ask for approval before applying any unfamiliar product to the finish. A material's general reputation for chemical resistance cannot tell you what will happen to painted features, glued components or a particular soft formulation.

## Understand the limits of a cleaning label

A cleaner's claim to remove dirt and a disinfectant's claim to kill specified organisms have different requirements. EPA explains that registered disinfectants need to be used according to their label, including the stated contact time. Compatibility with your figure still requires separate confirmation. [EPA guidance on cleaning and disinfecting products](https://www.epa.gov/coronavirus-and-disinfectants/whats-difference-between-products-disinfect-sanitize-and-clean)

Use one approved method at a time. Combining household chemicals or applying a second product over residue can create additional problems. CDC's general guidance advises using a suitable cleaning method and keeping chemicals separate. Do not turn a household disinfection instruction into a treatment for a painted companion surface without product-specific approval. [CDC safe-cleaning guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/when-and-how-to-clean-and-disinfect-your-home.html)

## Protect the finish between cleaning sessions

Inspect clothing on the inside, especially dark elastic, synthetic trim, printed areas and metal fasteners. Choose light, colour-fast fabrics and remove a new outfit periodically to check the contact areas. A washed garment can still transfer colour during prolonged contact. Store jewellery separately so its edges cannot rest against the surface.

Makeup and adhesive products need approval for the actual finish. Ask how the product should be removed before applying it. A cosmetic that looks good in a photograph is of limited use if its removal requires a solvent unsuitable for the figure. The [hair and makeup guide](/blog/wigs-makeup-and-surface-aftercare) covers those accessory decisions.

Keep the figure out of direct sunlight and away from heaters or a hot car. A comfortable storage room also needs clean supports with broad contact areas. Check the surface touching a chair edge, stand fitting, heel support or folded limb. The weight should be carried by an approved arrangement with adequate capacity.

## Treat longevity as an inspection record

A useful record describes the condition you can actually observe. Keep a few dated photographs of the neck, joint areas and supports under similar light. Note the cleaner used and any changes in clothing, pose or storage. This makes it easier to identify whether a mark appeared after one particular exposure.

For example, suppose a pale shadow appears beneath a new dark waistband. Remove the garment, photograph the affected surface and use only the normal approved cleaning method. Ask for guidance before trying a stain-removal compound. Scrubbing through the pigment or layering unapproved chemicals can leave a larger problem than the original mark.

A loose joint or opening seam needs a different report. Record where movement changed and avoid repeatedly testing the failure. Send photographs and the order reference through the agreed contact process. The [returns policy](/returns) explains how suspected factory defects and arrival damage are assessed. It does not provide an invented lifespan guarantee.

## Store for the next handling session

Before a long pause, clean and dry the figure according to its instructions. Choose a supported, relaxed pose and a suitable breathable cover. Keep dyes, sharp edges and heavy objects away from the surface. Follow any separate storage guidance for an electronic module or charger.

When bringing the figure out again, inspect the underside and contact points before moving it into a new pose. Check hair attachments, fingers and accessory fittings, then dress it in clean suitable clothing. Revisit the [posing and storage guide](/blog/posing-stands-and-storage) if the existing arrangement leaves pressure on a small area or makes safe handling difficult.

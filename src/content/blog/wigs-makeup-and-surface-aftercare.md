Style a companion figure by treating the hair, painted face, soft skin, and any electronics as separate components. Confirm the hair type and approved cleaning products before removing a wig, changing makeup, or applying an adhesive. Keep liquids, loose powder, and styling products away from seams, eyes, speakers, and ports.

A finished photograph may include a sample head, separate wig, clothing, jewelry, or makeup arranged for that shoot. Ask which of those items belongs to your order. Once the supplied configuration is clear, styling changes are easier to plan without risking the underlying surface.

## Identify the component before choosing a product

| Component | Confirm first | Practical approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Removable wig | Fiber, cap size, attachment, cleaning instructions | Perform approved cleaning and styling off the head |
| Implanted hair | Fiber type and approved root care | Support the roots and detangle gently |
| Painted silicone face | Permitted cleaner and finish | Use the smallest controlled amount directed |
| TPE or STPE skin | Approved care and cosmetic compatibility | Keep body renewal products separate from facial styling |
| Eyes and lashes | Whether removal or adjustment is intended | Follow the exact head instructions |
| Electronic head or voice box | Openings, seams, and power guidance | Keep styling materials away from components and connectors |

Start with the [care page](/care) and [materials guide](/blog/tpe-vs-stpe-vs-silicone) if the head and body's care requirements are still unclear.

## Confirm what the hair actually is

A wig can look convincingly rooted in a photograph. Ask whether the hair is removable, implanted synthetic hair, or implanted real hair, and which description applies to the offered head.

A silicone options sheet dated 4 October 2025 lists implanted hair at an additional $150 and implanted real hair at an additional $280. These figures are historical option references, with current availability and cost confirmed in the quotation. A photograph alone cannot identify the chosen option.

If a wig is supplied, ask about its fiber, cap, and fastening method. If the hair is implanted, ask for the approved washing and detangling instructions for both the fiber and the scalp material.

Keep that information with the order. It becomes useful later when buying replacement care products or explaining a concern about shedding, tangling, or the hairline.

## Remove and care for a wig only as instructed

Once removability is confirmed, support the head and follow the specified release method. A cap held by an adhesive or fastening system may need a different approach from a loosely fitted wig.

Place the removed wig on a clean surface or appropriate form. Begin gentle detangling at the ends while supporting the section above the knot, then work upward. Use the comb or brush recommended for that fiber and cap.

Follow the wig maker's washing and drying instructions. A hair product intended for human hair may be unsuitable for a synthetic fiber, and a care product for the wig does not automatically belong on the figure's skin.

Let the wig and cap dry fully before fitting them again. Keep the inside clean and check for staining, residue, or a rough fastening edge before it returns to contact with the head.

## Check heat guidance by fiber type

Some synthetic wigs are specifically designed for heat styling, while others have different limits. The label and instructions for the actual wig should determine the tools and settings used.

Jon Renau, for example, identifies its Heat model as heat-friendly synthetic and provides care directions for that particular fiber. This illustrates why heat compatibility should be established from a wig's own specification. [Jon Renau's fiber and care example](https://jonrenau.com/heat)

Even when a removed wig permits heat, protect the figure from the tool and hot fiber. Carry out the approved styling away from the silicone or elastomer head, painted finish, glue lines, and electronics.

If the wig's fiber or limit is unknown, ask before applying heat. A curling iron's normal setting for human hair provides no information about the cap, adhesive, or figure surface beneath it.

## Handle implanted hair at the roots

Implanted hair remains attached to the scalp, so combing can transfer force directly to the insertion points. Work in small sections, support the hair close to the roots, and use the method supplied for the head.

Avoid treating a tight hairstyle as harmless because it looks neat. Elastic bands, clips, and sustained tension can pull at the hairline. Ask which styling and washing methods are approved for the fitted hair.

Keep liquid controlled around the scalp, eyes, and neck. An electronic head needs particular care around openings and internal components. If the hair requires treatment that seems to conflict with the head instructions, resolve the question with support before proceeding.

Photograph unusual shedding or a change at the hairline while it can still be assessed. The [returns process](/returns) explains how to raise a defect concern before attempting a repair or re-rooting.

## Protect the painted face when changing makeup

The existing lips, eyebrows, lashes, and complexion may be part of a painted finish. Ask which areas can be cleaned or restyled and which products are approved for that specific surface.

A cosmetic described as gentle for human skin can still contain pigments, oils, or removers unsuitable for a figure's material or paint. Choose products on the basis of confirmed compatibility and a removal method that is also approved.

Where a maker recommends a small-area test, follow that method and observe the result before using the product more broadly. A short test still cannot guarantee that prolonged contact will remain colorfast or leave the finish unaffected.

Use separate clean tools for body care and facial styling. Apply a small controlled amount as directed instead of spraying directly onto the face. Keep residue away from the neck connection and hair attachments.

## Treat eyes and lashes according to the head design

Some heads permit eye adjustment or removal. Others have a different construction, and an electronic head can add moving mechanisms behind the visible surface.

Ask for the correct method before touching a fitted eye or lash. Work with clean hands and a supported head. Avoid using an eye socket as an opening through which to flush liquid or remove cosmetic residue.

If an eye moves unexpectedly, a lash loosens, or a component binds, stop and document it. A close photograph of the area and the head reference helps support provide a suitable response.

The [hybrid head guide](/blog/hybrid-silicone-head-and-tpe-body) is useful when the face and body use different materials. Keep the care instructions for each component identifiable.

## Keep electronic openings clear during styling

The [expression-head figure](/ai-robots/wm-164-f-1068) includes electronic components that need their own care and power guidance. Powder, oils, makeup, and water should be controlled around the display, microphone, speakers, seams, and connectors.

Turn the unit off for care as directed, and follow its instructions before disconnecting any component. Protect openings while treating nearby surfaces using only the approved method.

The optional [voice box](/voice-box) also needs its opening and connections protected during body care. Identify its location before applying a renewal product or fitting an outfit.

If liquid or residue reaches an electronic area, stop the styling task and request guidance before charging or testing. Record the product used and where it reached so the issue can be assessed accurately.

## Clothing and jewelry also affect the finish

A freshly styled face can draw attention away from a dark collar, tight bracelet, or rough zipper that remains against the skin. Check every new accessory for pressure, sharp edges, and unstable dyes.

Choose clean, fully dry clothing and inspect the contact areas regularly. Light, color-fast fabric is a sensible starting point. A clean cloth test can reveal loose pigment, while prolonged contact with the actual material can behave differently.

Keep metal fasteners and pins from pressing into the soft skin. Remove tight accessories for longer storage where the care instructions recommend it. The [posing and storage guide](/blog/posing-stands-and-storage) explains support and contact points.

## A simple styling session

Prepare the approved products, clean cloths, and a stable supported position. Confirm which hair can be removed, complete the wig care separately if permitted, and let everything dry.

Work on one area at a time so a change in color, tackiness, or finish can be traced to the product used. Keep the face, body treatments, and electronic openings separate throughout the session.

After refitting the hair and clothing, check the neckline, ears, hands, and any fastener. Save photographs of the finished result and the product names used. That record makes future styling and aftercare questions easier to answer.
